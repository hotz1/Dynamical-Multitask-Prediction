\subsection{Gaussian Processes}

A Gaussian process (GP) is a real-valued stochastic process indexed over some $D$-dimensional \emph{state space} $\calX \subseteq \bbR^{D}$ which satisfies the following key property:

\begin{definition}
    For any finite collection ($N$-tuple) of states $(x_{1}, \cdots, x_{N}) \subset \calX$, the joint distribution of the vector $\big(f(x_{1}), \cdots, f(x_{N})\big)$ follows a multivariate Gaussian distribution on $\bbR^{N}$. 
\end{definition}

Furthermore, every Gaussian process on the state space $\calX$ is fully specified by two real-valued functions on $\calX$, which respectively describe the mean and covariance of the underlying process \cite{GPML-Book-2006}. Formally, every Gaussian process defined on the underlying state space $\calX$ is \emph{uniquely} described by a mean function $\mu: \calX \to \bbR$ and a covariance (kernel) function $K: \calX \times \calX \to \bbR$ which provide the following structure:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mean:} $\mu(x) = \expec{Y(x)}$ for all $x \in \calX$. 
    \item \textbf{Covariance:} $K(x, x') = \covar{Y(x)}{Y(x')}$ for all $(x, x') \in \calX \times \calX$.
\end{itemize}
As every Gaussian process on $\calX$ is uniquely determined by these two functions, we may simply write that $f \sim \calGP(\mu, K)$ to denote a Gaussian process with mean $\mu$ and kernel $K$.

Due to these convenient properties, Gaussian processes are frequently used in a variety of practical statistical applications, ranging from geostatistics and climatology to fields such as electrical engineering and signal processing \citet{Sarkka-Spatial-Kalman-2012,Huang-Nonstationary-2025}.

Gaussian process regression, often referred to in the literature as \emph{kriging} after the South African geostatistician Danie G. Krige \citep{Cressie-Origins-1990,Meta-Kriging-2018}, is a semi-parametric statistical modelling procedure wherein a finite collection of observations are used for learning the outputs of an unknown function defined on the state space $\calX$.
